## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & OBJECTIVE

This briefing packet prepares external legal counsel (intellectual property litigation, contract law, and public international law practitioners) for review of the **Volnost Ethical Public License (VEPL v1.0)**.

The objective of VEPL is to create a legally enforceable, robust source-available / ethical public license that:
1. Grants maximum technological liberties (use, modification, inspection, distribution) symmetrically to all natural persons and organizations worldwide.
2. Invariant negative covenants restrict only state terror, aggressive warfare, war crimes, and mass electronic subjugation.
3. Anchors prohibited conduct strictly to objective statutory definitions of international criminal law (Rome Statute of the ICC, UN Convention Against Torture, UDHR).
4. Provides an explicit, non-derogable safe harbor for legitimate self-defense under Article 51 of the Charter of the United Nations.
5. Avoids the fatal legal vulnerabilities of early ethical licenses (vagueness, subjective creator discretion, and jurisdictional extraterritoriality).

## 3. CORE LEGAL INNOVATIONS & RISK MITIGATION

### 3.1. Elimination of Subjective Void-for-Vagueness
- *Vulnerability in Predecessors (e.g. Hippocratic License 1.0, JSON "shall not be used for evil"):* Phrases like "evil" or "harm" are subjective, unenforceable in civil and common law courts, and trigger contract invalidation under the doctrine of vagueness.
- *VEPL Solution:* Zero moralizing vocabulary. All prohibitions cite formal statutory articles of ratified international treaties. A breach occurs only when the conduct fulfills the statutory elements of crimes defined under the Rome Statute or UN conventions.

### 3.2. Safe Harbor for Legitimate Self-Defense (UN Charter Art. 51)
- *Risk:* Pacifist or anti-military clauses that forbid "any military use" render software unusable for sovereign states resisting armed aggression or protecting civilian populations.
- *VEPL Solution:* Section 4 explicitly carves out a statutory safe harbor. Defensive operations against armed aggression in accordance with Article 51 UN Charter are fully protected.

### 3.3. Protection of Downstream Recipients (Section 5.3)
- *Risk:* Cascade revocation where a violation by an upstream intermediary invalidates licenses of millions of innocent downstream civilian users.
- *VEPL Solution:* Good-faith downstream users remain fully licensed; only the bad-faith violating entity has its license suspended.

### 3.4. Dispute Resolution & Due Process (Section 5.2)
- *Mechanism:* 30-day notice-and-cure window. In case of contested facts, adjudication may be established before competent judicial forums or independent international arbitral tribunals (such as UNCITRAL rules).

## 4. OSD (OPEN SOURCE DEFINITION) DEMARCATION

Counsel should advise clients and stakeholders with complete honesty:
- Under **Open Source Definition (OSD) §§ 5 and 6**, an OSI-approved license must not discriminate against any person or field of endeavor (even warfare or criminal operations).
- Because VEPL prohibits aggressive warfare and war crimes, **VEPL v1.0 is intentionally NOT marketed as "OSI-Approved Open Source"**.
- It is classified as an **Ethical Public License / Source-Available Software Covenant**.
